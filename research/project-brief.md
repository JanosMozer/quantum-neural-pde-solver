## Project brief — 2026-07-30 (canonical, supersedes scattered discussion)

### I. What we are
A two-person collaboration (you + peer) targeting one joint short paper/technical note in ~3.5 weeks, in the specific niche of "Quantum-Train"-style architectures (a parameterized quantum circuit generates classical neural-network weights, then the quantum circuit is discarded and only the classical weights ship) applied to solving a PDE via a physics-informed neural network (PINN), with 2D Burgers' equation as the concrete testbed.

### II. Division of labor
- **Peer:** architects and trains the QT-PINN on 2D Burgers' — gets the generator (PQC → weight-mapping layer → classical PINN weights) working and converging. This is implementation work; they own it.
- **You:** (a) a short warm-up study confirming a known dequantization failure boundary empirically (builds hands-on grounding before the harder case); (b) the main contribution — classify the chosen ansatz against known classical-simulability criteria, state an explicit prediction, then build two matched-parameter-count classical weight-generator baselines and run a head-to-head ablation against the peer's trained quantum generator.

### III. Result (the deliverable)
A short joint paper answering, for a real PDE task (not toy classification): **does a Quantum-Train-style weight generator do anything a classical weight generator can't, at matched parameter count?** Concretely: a classification verdict (is the ansatz inside or outside the known classically-simulable regime), a stated a priori prediction, and an empirical ablation table that confirms or refutes it. Secondary, standalone deliverable: an empirical confirmation of the RFF-dequantization failure boundary from Sweke et al. (Phase 0), useful even if the main result stalls.

### IV. How — the concrete plan (~18 working days)
- **Phase 0 (≈4 days, warm-up):** build a PQC data-reuploading regressor with uniform/product-induced frequency re-weighting (the exact construction Sweke et al.'s Lemma 3 proves RFF must fail on); build a matched classical RFF baseline (sklearn); measure required feature count M vs. problem dimension d; check against the theorem's predicted super-polynomial scaling.
- **Phase 1 (≈5 days, build + classify):** implement our own copy of the QT-PINN independent of the peer's repo pace — classical Fourier front-end, strongly-entangling non-local (circular-CNOT) PQC, weight-mapping linear layer, physics-informed loss, Adam→L-BFGS. Test both readout schemes (the peer's cheap per-qubit ⟨Z_i⟩, and the literal full-2^N-distribution readout from the actual QT paper — these are not the same thing, worth resolving with the peer). Classify the ansatz's entanglement/locality structure against Cerezo et al.'s light-cone-reduction + classical-shadow criteria → a specific, falsifiable a priori prediction. Build two classical baseline generators at matched parameter count: random low-rank projection (cheap), MPS/tensor-network via `quimb` (theory-matched, stronger).
- **Phase 2 (≈5 days, ablation):** train all three generators identically (same data, loss, optimizer budget) on the same 2D Burgers' instance. Measure PDE residual, IC/BC loss, held-out-region generalization, cross-seed stability. Check whether the outcome matches Phase 1's prediction.
- **Phase 3 (≈4 days, writeup):** assemble as a short technical note, explicitly citing and distinguishing from the three 2026 near-miss papers (section VI). Reconcile with whatever the peer has actually trained by then. Finalize `ours/qm-foundations.md`.

### V. Why it's novel — stated without overselling
The exact combination (generate-then-discard quantum weight generation, applied to a PDE/PINN task, evaluated by matched-parameter-count ablation against a classical generator, informed by an a priori classical-simulability classification) has not been done. This was checked, not assumed: two independent adversarial literature searches, ~19 combined search angles, full text checked for every candidate — including whether the mechanism's own inventors (who extended Quantum-Train to 8+ other domains across 2024-2026: LSTM/flood, RL, federated learning, tensor-network variants, deepfake-audio, multi-agent RL, typhoon forecasting, architecture search) ever tried the PDE extension. They haven't.

**What this is NOT claiming:** a dramatic "quantum wins" reveal. Given the already-established pattern in the literature — barren-plateau-avoiding ansätze repeatedly turn out classically simulable, confirmed across multiple unrelated ansatz families by Cerezo et al. and others — the more likely outcome of the ablation is "no measurable advantage," which extends an already-confirmed pattern to one more untested case rather than discovering something new. The actual novelty sits in two places: (1) being the *first rigorous check* of this specific, real (non-toy) application, where the answer genuinely wasn't known before, and (2) the reusable classify-then-predict-then-ablate *methodology*, independent of which way the result lands. Overstating the empirical surprise would be dishonest; understating the methodological and due-diligence value would undersell real work.

### VI. Related work

- **Cerezo et al. 2023**, arXiv:2312.09121 — classical simulability of barren-plateau-avoiding ansätze via light-cone reduction + classical shadows (second theoretical lens, used for the a priori classification).
- **Sweke et al. 2023/2024**, arXiv:2309.11647 — RFF dequantization necessity/sufficiency theorem, basis for the Phase 0 warm-up.
- **McClean et al. 2018** (1803.11173), **Cerezo et al. 2021** (2001.00550) — foundational barren-plateau scaling results, background only.
- **Three 2026 near-miss papers, must cite and explicitly differentiate from:** arXiv:2505.09395 (typhoon forecasting — has the QT mechanism and a classical-baseline ablation, wrong domain and wrong baseline family); arXiv:2606.04679 (hybrid QC-PINN on Burgers'/Allen-Cahn/KdV — right domain, wrong mechanism: circuit stays live at inference); arXiv:2606.18713 (Photonic Quantum Neural Fields for PINN, 7 PDE families incl. ### VII. The attack, precisely
Object: a classical weight-generator for a 2D Burgers'-PINN. Input class: a fixed non-1D-local (circular-CNOT), shallow, small-angle-init PQC ansatz, N∈[8,14] qubits (sized to the peer's own RTX 5090 compute estimates). Procedure: (1) classify the ansatz's light-cone depth/entanglement structure against Cerezo et al.'s criteria → a specific, falsifiable prediction; (2) build matched-parameter-count classical alternatives (random low-rank projection; MPS via `quimb`); (3) train all three generators identically; (4) measure PDE residual, IC/BC loss, out-of-distribution generalization, cross-seed variance; (5) check whether the empirical outcome matches the prediction. Output: a quantitative comparison table plus a stated resolution of whether the a priori classification was correct.

### VIII. Downstream effects — scoped honestly, not oversold
1. **Peer's paper (certain, immediate):** the result directly determines the honest claim available — "real advantage for this PDE class" (positive, genuinely notable since no toy-classification precedent generalizes to real PDE tasks) vs. "quantum-inspired compression trick, empirically indistinguishable from a classical low-rank/MPS generator at matched parameters" (negative — still legitimate and citable, and the *more likely* branch given the prior pattern).
2. **Scientific-ML/PDE-surrogate engineering — conditional, flagged as the less likely branch:** if positive, a real usable compression/regularization lever for CFD surrogates, structural/thermal simulation, real-time PDE-based control. This is not the expected outcome and should not be the paper's headline bet.
3. **QML methodology — fairly confident, low-conditionality:** the classify-then-predict-then-ablate procedure is directly reusable as an audit checklist for the growing "quantum generates classical X" paper family (QTRL, QT-LoRA/ICLR2025, the federated/MARL/typhoon QT extensions, and future ones). Real infrastructure value regardless of which way any single ablation lands — the same role Cerezo et al.'s and the QCNN-simulability papers already play for the field.
4. **Dequantization literature — small, standalone, low-risk:** Phase 0 produces one more concrete, cleanly verified empirical instance of Sweke et al.'s theoretical failure boundary. Minor but real and citable — nobody had run this specific numerical confirmation.
5. **Explicit non-claims:** no complexity-theoretic quantum-advantage claim is being made or challenged (that's Liu et al.'s separate, pre-existing proof). No generality beyond the tested ansatz/task is claimed — any result is evidence for this one instance (gap type C), not a new theorem. This is not being positioned as a "quantum wins" paper; the honest prior, stated up front, favors a negative-or-null result on the advantage question, with the paper's real value in rigor, methodology, and protecting the peer's claim — not in a dramatic reveal.
