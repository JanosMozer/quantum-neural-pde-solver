**Feynman pass — what is "Quantum-Train" actually doing?**

Forget the word "quantum" for a second. You have a small vector of numbers (`θ`, the circuit's rotation angles). You run them through some fixed nonlinear machinery and get out N numbers (`⟨Z_i⟩`, one per qubit). You then feed those N numbers through a plain linear layer to blow them up into thousands of classical neural-network weights. That's it — that's the whole trick, structurally identical to a hypernetwork or a random-feature expansion: few numbers in, many correlated numbers out, via a fixed nonlinear map.

The "quantum" part is the specific nonlinear machinery: the N numbers you read out aren't arbitrary — they're expectation values of a state that's constrained to live on the unit sphere of a `2^N`-dimensional complex Hilbert space, reached by a *unitary* (norm-preserving, invertible) transformation. That constraint is exactly why the output is smooth and doesn't blow up: you can't accidentally produce weights with runaway magnitude the way an untrained random matrix might, because unitarity keeps everything bounded and structured.
